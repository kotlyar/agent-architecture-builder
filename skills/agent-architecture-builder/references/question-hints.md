# Optional answer hints

Hints help a person start when recalling or listing work is difficult. They are
examples, not requirements and not architecture decisions.

## When to offer hints

Offer a short card when the user asks for examples, says “I do not know,” gives
an answer too broad to examine, or must recall several kinds of work, results,
control methods, or stored information. Build options from the user's words and
domain. Do not use a prewritten industry checklist when the context already
supports more relevant examples.

Do not offer options instead of a factual value, deadline, budget, account name,
or exact approval. Do not suggest an agent, database, orchestrator, or browser
panel before the need is established.

## Card format

Ask one simple question, explain that several options or a free answer are
allowed, show three to seven concrete options, and include “I am not sure yet.”
Each option must describe observable work or a result with an ordinary verb. Do
not mix the work with its implementation method or autonomy level.

After a selection, ask for the most important item in the first version and
reconstruct one real or expected episode. Selected boxes are stated needs, not
proof of separate skills or agents.

## Why the system is needed

> What do you mainly want to improve? You may choose several or write your own
> answer.

1. Reduce repetitive manual work.
2. Make quality more consistent.
3. Make decisions or delivery faster.
4. Stop losing tasks and context between sessions.
5. Coordinate several kinds of work.
6. Reduce risk before external actions.
7. I am not sure yet.

## Work in the named area

Generate this card from the user's own area. Use observable stages such as:

1. Collect and check source information.
2. Analyze the situation and identify problems.
3. Prepare a recommendation or plan.
4. Create a draft or other work product.
5. Check quality against agreed criteria.
6. Apply an approved change.
7. Measure the result and propose the next decision.
8. I am not sure yet.

Replace these generic stages with the user's vocabulary. Do not introduce work
the user did not mention unless it is clearly labeled as an optional example.

## Level of independence

> How would you like to supervise this work at first?

1. The assistant gathers information and shows it to me.
2. The assistant proposes a decision and I choose.
3. The assistant prepares an exact draft and I approve execution.
4. The assistant acts within limits agreed in advance.
5. Different actions need different rules.
6. I am not sure yet.

A high-level choice is not authorization for a risky action. Record exact
targets, accounts, content, limits, and approval rules separately.

## Control interface

> Where would it be easiest to assign work and review the result?

1. In a normal conversation.
2. In a messenger with tasks and notifications.
3. In a browser panel with a queue, statuses, artifacts, and approvals.
4. In a command line.
5. Through an API used by another system.
6. Through a combination of these.
7. I am not sure yet.

Follow with who uses it, how often, whether several people share state, what must
be approved, and whether mobile access matters.

## What must be remembered

> What information should still be available next week or next month?

1. Goals and success criteria.
2. Current and completed work.
3. Decisions and their evidence.
4. Versions of materials or changes.
5. Measurements and actual outcomes.
6. Approvals, failures, and execution attempts.
7. User preferences.
8. I am not sure yet.

After the selection, establish owner, retention, search, privacy, concurrent
editing, and audit needs. Do not choose a database by counting selected items.

## What must be configured before work

> What would prevent the assistant from producing its first useful result? You
> may choose several items or write your own.

1. Access to the working site, folder, or source files.
2. A connection to the service that holds source data.
3. An account with the intended scope and read permissions.
4. A safely stored access key or token.
5. The project, site, account, or workspace identifier.
6. A human decision that cannot be derived automatically.
7. Nothing: message-provided data is sufficient for the first version.
8. I am not sure yet.

After the selection, establish the setup owner, safe value location, and an
observable check. Never ask for a password or token in chat. When required setup
is absent, design setup mode rather than domain work based on guesses.

## First complete result

When the user names many kinds of work, generate three to five candidate first
results only from those named items. Each candidate must run from a clear input
to a verifiable outcome and be useful on its own.

## Record

Save the exact question, shown options, selections, free answer, what a work
episode confirmed, and what remains a hypothesis. This distinguishes user
evidence from suggestions introduced by the interviewer.
